Groove Privacy Policy

Last updated October 22, 2026

Groove is a movement mentorship and education app run by Greg Spicer. This page explains, in plain words, what Groove collects and how it is used.

What Groove Collects

  • Account details: your email, your name and photo (if you add them), and your password (stored securely by our sign-in provider, never visible to Greg). If you sign in with Google, we receive only your name and email address from Google.
  • Workout and fitness information: workouts you log, weekly goals, body measurements you choose to enter (such as age, weight, height and heart rate), and fitness test results.
  • Health check answers: the short pre-exercise screening and any injuries or conditions you choose to share, so workouts can be suggested more safely.
  • Journal entries: private. Greg cannot read them.
  • Strava (optional): if you connect Strava, Groove imports your workouts from it.
  • Reminders (optional): if you turn on notifications, your device's notification address is stored so reminders can reach you.

Who Can See It

  • Greg can see your account details, workouts, goals and health check answers so he can offer mentorship. He cannot see your journal.
  • Our service providers handle the data for us: Supabase (database and sign-in), Vercel (hosting) and Stripe (payments). Stripe handles your card details on its own pages. Groove never sees or stores your card number.
  • We do not sell your information, and we do not share it for advertising.

How It Is Used

To run the app, track your progress, suggest workouts, send reminders you asked for, process your membership, and let Greg offer mentorship. Nothing else.

Your Choices

  • You can edit your information in Account at any time.
  • You can delete your account and everything stored with it from Account > Delete Account. You can also email the address below and we will do it for you.
  • You can disconnect Strava or turn off reminders at any time.

Not Medical Advice

Groove provides movement tracking and education. It is not medical advice. Check with your doctor before starting a new physical activity program, especially if you have a health condition.
